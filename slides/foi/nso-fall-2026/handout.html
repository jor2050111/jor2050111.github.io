Computer and Information Technology

You picked an interest, not a career. Here is what that interest can turn into, and what to do first.

Few community colleges can award a bachelor's degree. This one can.

Start Certificates and your first coursesShort, stackable, and employable on their own. CompTIA A+ is the usual first target.
Next Associate degree (AAS)Two years. Everything you take counts toward the next step.
Then Bachelor of Applied Science in Information Technology120 credits across eight terms. Phoenix College teaches every course, upper division included, and awards the degree. You do not transfer out to finish.

Jobs you have probably never heard of

  • Site reliability engineer. Keeps large systems running, and stays calm when they break.
  • Cloud cost engineer. Finds the money a company wastes on cloud services.
  • Security operations analyst. Decides which alerts are real attacks, then runs the response.
  • Data engineer. Builds the pipes that carry data to the people analyzing it.

Get involved from week one

Computer Science Club. Mondays at noon, E-223. PC Business Club. Tuesdays at 1:00, B-317. Bear's Den. The college pitch competition, for anyone building something of their own. Campus student worker roles. Paid, on campus, and directly industry-relevant.

Five things to do in your first eight weeks

  1. Go to office hours in week two, before anything is wrong. Introduce yourself. That is the whole visit.
  2. Open a GitHub account this week. Every piece of work you make goes in it. In two years it beats a resume.
  3. Join the Computer Science Club. Those people become your study group first and your network later.
  4. Pick one certification target and keep it in view. CompTIA A+ is the usual first one.
  5. Keep a file of what you built and one sentence on why you built it that way. You will need it at your first interview.
