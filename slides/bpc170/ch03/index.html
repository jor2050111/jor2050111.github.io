Check: Size the supply

The parts add up to 380 W. Which supply ships?

400 W unit550 W unit

Commit first: The build sheet’s components draw 380 W at full load. Pick a supply and defend the number.

The 550 W unit. Add 10-15% overhead to the 380 W draw and the build needs 418 to 437 W. The 400 W supply is over budget on day one. The sheet takes the 550, and its 80 Plus rating tells you how little power it wastes as heat.

BPC170 · 3.2
Storage devices

No moving parts, no waiting on a spinning platter.

Hard disk drive
  • Magnetic platters, spindle, heads on an actuator
  • 5,400 / 7,200 RPM desktops, 10K or 15K in servers
  • Lower cost 3.5″ or 2.5″, SATA interface
Solid-state drive
  • No moving parts non-magnetic flash
  • Faster reads and writes than any platter
  • Higher cost for the same capacity

Judge a drive on reliability, performance, and use. The build takes one of each: fast system drive, roomy data drive.

Bigger arrays

Parity buys safety without mirroring every byte.

RAID 5
D·DD·PP·D

Single parity, spread out. 3-drive minimum. Fast read, slower write.

✓ survives 1
RAID 6
D·PD·PP·DP·D

Double parity. 4-drive minimum. Less space, degraded with two down.

✓ survives 2
RAID 10
1·31·32·42·4

A stripe of mirrors. 4-drive minimum, always an even count.

✓ 1 per mirror

D marks data, P marks parity. Parity is the math that rebuilds a dead drive’s blocks.

BPC170 · 3.2
Check: Pick the level

Commit: which RAID protects the project archive?

The constraint: The studio’s archive box has room for exactly two drives, and a failed drive must never take the projects with it. RAID 0, 1, or 5?

RAID 1. Mirroring survives a single failed drive with only two disks. RAID 5 wants at least three, and RAID 0 dies with its first failure. The price is capacity: two drives, one drive’s worth of space.
