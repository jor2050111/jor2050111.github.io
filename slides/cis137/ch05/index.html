Quick Check 5.1

Describe the style, or show it?

A city recreation center wants its weekly class listings in one fixed style. Decide whether to describe the style in words or show two past listings, and justify your choice with the library reminder result.

Show two past listings. In the library run, two samples did what a paragraph of style rules would struggle to do. The tool copied the order, the short day and time, the bars, and the plain ending, and it dropped the extra promise.
CIS137 · 5.2
Fix It 5.1 · The build night update

Right content, wrong shape. Add a Format part, nothing else.

Before

Symptom: an email, bound for a group text

**Subject: Thursday Build Night Time Change**

Hi everyone,

Quick update: this Thursday's build night has moved from 5:00 p.m. to **6:30 p.m.** We'll be in the same room as usual.

Please remember to bring your safety glasses, as you won't be able to work in the build area without them.

See you Thursday!

[Your name] [Robotics Club]

After

Repair: the request, word for word, plus Format

[...] everyone should bring safety glasses. Format: one plain-text message for our club's group text, under 30 words, with no subject line and no bold or other formatting. Reply with the message only.

Verify: paste it as is

Robotics club: Thursday's build night moved from 5 p.m. to 6:30 p.m. Same room as before. Please bring your safety glasses. See you there!

24 words under a 30-word limit

Sample, Oct 2026. Your output will differ.

A missing Format part does not always fail. Check every time.

CIS137 · 5.2
Quick Check 5.2

What can the written checks prove?

Analyze what the youth sports response's written checks let you verify that a one-line answer would not, and name the one thing the checks cannot prove.

Each check is a line you can test in seconds: 5:30 plus 75 minutes is 6:45, past the 6:30 cutoff. A one-line answer gives you only a verdict. The checks cannot prove how the model worked. They are text it produced, and a neat chain can still hold a wrong step.

CIS137 · 5.3
Strategy 7 · Grounding

Is it in the source? That you can check.

  1. Supply the source the answer must come from.
  2. Tell the model to answer only from it.
  3. Say what to do when the source is silent.

“Is this true?” becomes “is this in the source?”

Strategy 9 · Negative constraints and guardrails

A guardrail stops only what it names.

Negative constraint: tells the model what not to do.

Guardrail: a negative constraint aimed at a harm you can name.

The guardrailWhat it namedWhat happened

Landscaping postDo not add prices, discounts, or services that are not listed.

Prices, discounts, services None added

A benefit got through: “so your yard gets just the water it needs” Not in source

Veterinary clinicDo not add medical claims or promises beyond these facts.

Medical claims, promises

Written from the persona run’s two additions: “won't feel or remember anything,” “he'll be in good hands”

Write guardrails from the failures you have seen.

CIS137 · 5.3
Quick Check 5.3

Could the clinic send it as written?

The veterinary technician response said "he won't feel or remember anything." Decide whether the clinic could send it as written, and write the one guardrail that would prevent claims like it.

Not as written. It is a medical claim the facts never made, and “he’ll be in good hands” is a promise. The guardrail: “Do not add medical claims or promises beyond these facts.”

CIS137 · 5.4
Quick Check 5.4

Three changes, five checks. What do you know?

A teammate repaired a prompt by adding grounding, a word limit, and a persona at once, and the score rose from two checks to five. Analyze what the repair sheet can and cannot conclude about why, and describe the runs that would settle it.

It can show the prompt got better, not why. Three changes in one run cannot be told apart. Settle it with one change per run, each in a fresh conversation, each scored. Then repeat a run, because two runs of one prompt differ by chance.
