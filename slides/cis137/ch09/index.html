CIS137 · 9.1
Where AI is strong and where it is weak

Patterns repeat. Judgment needs facts and someone who answers.

Strong: pattern work at volume
  • Sorting many similar items into categories you define
  • First drafts of routine messages
  • Summaries of text you supply
  • Translation of plain instructions
Weak: judgment and accountability
  • Judgment that depends on facts it was never given
  • Fresh facts after its knowledge cutoff
  • Long chains of reasoning where one slip spreads
  • Accountability: it cannot answer for a decision

A starting point, not a law. Test each use.

Quick Check 9.1

Translate the pool rules or deny the permits?

A city parks department wants AI to translate its pool rules into Vietnamese and to decide which park permit requests to deny. Sort each use as strong or weak, and name the check a person must run on the one you would allow.

Translating the pool rules is strong: translation of plain instructions is pattern work. Deciding which permits to deny is weak: it needs facts the tool lacks and a person who answers for it. Allow the translation, and have a staff member who reads Vietnamese fluently check it before it posts.
CIS137 · 9.2
The Use Evaluation

“Should we use AI?” breaks into five factors you can measure.

  1. CostMonthly price per user, plus setup time
  2. Time savedMinutes saved per task times tasks, after checking
  3. Error costWhat one mistake costs when it gets through
  4. RiskWhat could go wrong, which rule, which control
  5. Workforce impactWhat changes for the people doing the work

Augmentation AI takes part of the task. Judgment and accountability stay.

Quick Check 9.2

Show each step. Then name the hole.

An apartment management office answers about 200 maintenance requests a week, at about 5 minutes each. In a pilot, 40 of 50 AI drafts needed only a light edit, at about 2 minutes. Calculate the weekly minutes the light-edit drafts would save, show each step, and name one number the estimate is missing.

5 − 2 = 3 minutes saved per light edit. 40 of 50 is 160 of 200 a week. 160 × 3 = 480 minutes a week, gross. Missing: the minutes to rewrite the drafts that were not light edits (or what one error costs).
