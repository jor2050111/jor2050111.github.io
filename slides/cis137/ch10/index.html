Quick Check 10.1

A $500 flag and a reorder tip.Which AI, which check?

A museum gift shop's point-of-sale system flags any sale over $500 for a manager's approval, and it also suggests which items to reorder from last year's sales. Classify each feature by the type of AI behind it, and name the check each one needs.

The $500 flag is rule-based: a fixed threshold triggers a fixed action. Check it by reading the rule. It replaces no judgment, so by this book’s test it is not AI. The reorder tip is machine learning that predicts from past sales. Count its wrong suggestions, the way a pilot counts errors.

CIS137 · 10.3
Quick Check 10.3

“23 points to 21.” What does the total hide?

A teammate adds the six columns into one total and reports "Tool A: 23 points, Tool B: 21 points." Critique the report, and rewrite the top line of it in counts and findings.

A total hides which criterion decided, and it lets strong counts buy back a fail. A privacy fail is not offset by three extra holds. Keep each cell a count or a finding, for example: “Task 1: both 4 of 4 checks in two runs. Tool B ahead by one Not in source claim.”
CIS137 · 10.4
Keeping the survey current

A matrix is trueon the day you ran it.

Tool A’s cardLast tested: Task 1 (printer-offline first reply), October 21, 2026. Capability 4 of 4 checks in both runs. Accuracy 5 claims checked, 4 hold, 1 not in source, in both runs

  1. The model or plan changes. The scores came from the old one.
  2. The data policy or terms change. The privacy cell may flip.
  3. The task changes. A new ticket type is a new matrix.
  4. Someone reports a failure. One report is a lead.
  5. Last tested passes the review period. Copperwind’s: three months.

Quick Check 10.4

Read 11 months ago. New plan last week.Test now or later?

A university housing office's card for its general assistant shows a data policy line read 11 months ago, and the vendor announced a new plan last week. Decide whether the tool gets the fair test again now or at the next review, and justify your answer with the re-test triggers.

Now. Two triggers fire. A new plan can change the terms, so the privacy cell may flip. A policy line read 11 months ago is past a review period like Copperwind’s three months. For records FERPA may cover, privacy is a must-pass, so it cannot wait.
