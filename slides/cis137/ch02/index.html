Knowing versus predicting

Same confident tone. Different evidence underneath.

The year of the first Moon landing Pattern: strong Seen millions of times. Prediction and truth line up. Confident
The closing time of a small shop near you Pattern: weak Seen rarely or never. It reaches for what closing times usually look like. Confident

The model has no separate signal that says “this one is a guess.”

Why the same prompt gives different answers

One different pick early changes every word after it.

Run 2DogGoneSonoran

For a name, chance is a gift. Run it five times and pick the best.

For a fact, chance is a warning. If answers conflict, at most one can be right.

Fix It 2.1 · Repair and verify

The repair made the failure visible. It did not make it true.

Before

Write a 100-word paragraph for a charter school's parent newsletter about how four-day school weeks affect attendance. Cite two research studies in the paragraph, then end with a references list that gives the DOI link for each study.

Result: a blended citation, and a claim of “fewer missed appointments” the studies do not make.

After
Step 1: the job, the audience, the size

Write a 100-word paragraph for a charter school's parent newsletter about how four-day school weeks affect attendance.

Step 2: forbid what is unsafe from memory

Do not include any links or DOI numbers.

Step 3: details a reader can look up

Cite two research studies by author, year, title, and journal, so I can find them myself.

Step 4: make doubt visible

After the paragraph, list any detail you are not sure of.

  • Holds Thompson (2021) and Morton (2023) both exist
  • Holds Morton: online June 2022, print March 2023
  • Contradicted “some districts report fewer absences” Chapter: “no support from that citation”

Claims first

  • Numbers: amounts, counts, percentages, prices, dates
  • Citations and links: every source, quote, and URL
  • Changes and causes: anything that moved, ended, or happened because
