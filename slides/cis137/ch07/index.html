CIS137 · 7.2
Where errors come from

An audit names what failed. The cause names the fix.

  1. 1Training datafills a gap with what is typical
  2. 2Prompt ambiguitythe prompt reads two ways, and the model picks one
  3. 3Reasoning gapsa count, date, or calculation step fails unseen
  4. 4Dated knowledgethe fact changed after the model’s knowledge cutoff

Quick Check 7.2

They asked for there and back. They got one way.

A community theater's touring crew asks for "the drive time to Flagstaff and back" and gets the one-way time. Name the cause, and write the one sentence you would add to the prompt.

Prompt ambiguity. The request could be read two ways, and the model picked one without saying so. Define the term (say round trip), or ask the model to ask. Add: “If anything in the request could mean two things, ask before you answer.”
CIS137 · 7.3
Where bias enters

Accurate line by line can still treat people unequally.

Bias: a consistent tilt for or against some people, unrelated to the task.

  1. DataThe examples over-represent some people and under-represent others.
  2. LabelsThe people who marked the examples brought their own judgments.
  3. DesignBuilders chose what to measure, what to optimize, and what counts as success.
  4. UseA person applies the tool to a job it was not built for, or trusts it where it is weak.

Most bias shows up only when you compare responses.

CIS137 · 7.3
The paired prompt test

Change one detail. Hold everything else still.

  1. One detail varies. Write it down before you run.
  2. Everything else is identical, word for word.
  3. Fresh conversations for every run.
  4. Criteria first. Decide what you compare before you read.
  5. More than one pair. One pair is a lead, not a finding.

Quick Check 7.3

“Glad” in one reply, “happy” in another. Does it need a ruling?

A paired test of a credit union's loan-inquiry reply shows the same facts and requirements in four runs, with "We'd be glad to help" in one reply and "Happy to help" in another. Decide whether the difference needs a fairness decision, and justify your answer in two sentences.

Probably not a problem: the facts and requirements matched in all four runs, so only the wording changed. Still, ruling that wording may vary is itself the fairness decision, and a person with authority makes it and writes it down.
CIS137 · 7.3
Fairness is a decision

Fairness is a decision a person writes down.

It is not a setting in a tool. Ways to measure it can disagree.

  • A rental replyWording may vary. Facts, requirements, and invitations may not.
  • A hiring summaryNothing about the applicant’s name may change the summary at all.

The audit reports what changed. A person with authority decides.

CIS137 · 7.4
Recommendation

Pick the recommendation by what the findings would cost.

  1. Use as isNo claim Contradicted, nothing Not in source that matters to the reader, and no bias finding.
  2. Fix and useThe failures are known, and a named change to the prompt or a human edit removes them.The dispatcher’s pick
  3. Do not useA failure the prompt cannot fix, or a bias finding about a person with no fix you can test.

Name the conditions. “Fix and use” without them leaves the manager guessing what to fix.
