CIS137 · 8.1
The four kinds of rules

Three questions sort any rule onto the shelf.

The ruleA credit union branch lets staff use one approved chat assistant, on a work account only.

  1. Who made it?An organization
  2. Who must follow it?Its own people
  3. What happens if it is broken?Discipline, lost access, or a lost job
  1. LawBinding, with penalties, made by a legislature or regulator
  2. PolicyAn organization’s own rules for its peopleThe rule lands here
  3. GuidanceA framework an organization chooses to follow
  4. EthicsThe principles behind the other three

Then ask: what kind is it today, and what points to it?

Quick Check 8.1

No EU customers. Does the Act bind it?

A trucking dispatcher in Phoenix has no EU customers, hires, or drivers. Its owner asks whether the EU AI Act’s high-risk rules apply to its applicant-ranking tool. Use Article 2(1)(c) to decide, and name the kind of rule the Act becomes for this owner.

No. Article 2(1)(c) reaches companies outside the EU only when the AI system’s output is used in the EU, and this dispatcher has no EU hires, customers, or drivers. For this owner it reads the Act for ideas, not as a law it must follow.

“I deleted the chat.” Is the data gone?

A coworker says, "I deleted the chat, so the client's data is gone." Using the fictional policy excerpt and the Tool check above, examine whether that claim holds, and say what your reading sheet should record.

Not fully. The chat leaves your history right away, but the vendor’s systems only within 30 days, and a flagged chat stays up to two years. One vendor keeps chats that people reviewed longer still. CIS137 · 8.3
Quick Check 8.3

A 20-word prompt, one pick, no edits. What can it claim?

A community theater’s volunteer writes a 20-word prompt, picks one of three generated logos, and changes nothing. The board wants to register the logo. Apply Part 2’s conclusions to decide what the theater can claim, and say what it would need to add to claim more.

Nothing in the bare logo. Prompts alone do not make the volunteer an author, and picking one of three adds no human expression. To claim more, the theater must add its own expression: its own changes to the image, or a layout and arrangement it made.

Quick Check 8.4

Back to the leasing office. Does HIPAA fit?

A draft rule reads "Staff must follow HIPAA when using AI [law]." Judge whether the rule and its label work for an apartment management office, and write the line the office should use instead.

Neither works. HIPAA covers health information held by covered entities and their business associates, and a leasing office is neither, so the [law] label points to the wrong document. Use a [policy] line instead: Never paste a tenant’s name, unit number, or other personal data into any AI tool.
