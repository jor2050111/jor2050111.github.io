CIS137 · 4.1
The bare prompt

Leave a fact out and the model hands back a bracket.

  • ContextEvery bracket: a fact she knew and did not say.
  • FormatEmoji and bold labels do not belong in a text.
  • ConstraintsA tip and a call line she never said.
CIS137 · 4.1
Five parts, five tests

Each part answers a question the model would otherwise guess.

  1. Role

    Whose knowledge, habits, and voice to borrow

    Could you name the job title the model is playing?

  2. Task

    The one action and the one thing it produces

    Does the prompt hold one verb and one deliverable?

  3. Context

    The facts, audience, and situation it cannot guess

    Could a stranger do the job with only these facts?

  4. Format

    The shape of the output: length, layout, structure

    Could you sketch the output's shape before you send?

  5. Constraints

    The limits: what must not happen and what must survive

    Does the prompt say what the model must not do?

The same message, built from parts

Every bracket became a fact you supplied.

  1. Role

    You are the front desk coordinator at a small family dental office.

  2. Task

    Write a text message to patients who have appointments next week.

  3. Context

    The office parking lot will be repaved Monday through Wednesday, March 9 to 11. On those days, patients can park free in the church lot across the street on Cedar Avenue. Our entrance and hours do not change.

  4. Format

    Send one message, under 300 characters, in plain text with no emojis.

  5. Constraints

    Do not add any facts that are not in this prompt, and do not ask patients to reply.

CIS137 · 4.2
Five prompt types

Choose the type by what you need back.

Instruction
returns the finished thing itself.
Question
returns an explanation, so you understand.
Completion
returns a continuation in your voice.
Role play
returns turns in character, for practice.
Example-led
returns a new item that copies the examples.
CIS137 · 4.2
Completion and role play

Completion fills the gaps. Role play needs a stop line.

CIS137 · 4.2
Quick Check 4.2

Practice for new volunteers. Which type?

A public library branch wants its new desk volunteers to practice telling a patron about a lost-item fee. Choose the prompt type, and justify your choice against the instruction type in two sentences.

Role play. The job is practice, and role play returns turns in character for the volunteer to answer. An instruction returns a finished script to read, not a conversation to rehearse. Add a stop line such as “Do not answer for me.”
