CIS137 · 1.1
The judgment test

Rules alone are not AI. Rules that replace a judgment are.

Not AI: rules, no judgment replaced
  • Parking gate opens when a paid ticket is scanned
  • Payroll pays time-and-a-half after 40 hours
  • Library system charges a fine the day a book is overdue
the line this book draws
AI: rules stand in for a judgment
  • 1970s medical expert system suggests a diagnosis from a list of symptoms

Quick Check 1.1

Two hospital programs. Which type is each?

A hospital’s scheduling program books the first open slot for every request. A radiology program flags X-rays that look like fractures after studying thousands of labeled images. Name the type of each, and name the one question that tells them apart.

The scheduler is a rule-based system. It replaces no judgment, so by this book’s test it is not AI. The radiology program is machine learning: it learned from labeled images. The question that tells them apart: where does the behavior come from?

Narrow and general

Every tool you can use today is narrow.

A chat assistant covers many topics. It still cannot reliably:

  • plan a long task on its own
  • check its own facts
  • learn a job it was never trained for

The photo app that finds your dog cannot sort your mail.

CIS137 · 1.2
AI winters

Twice the promises outran the results.

  1. BreakthroughExpert systems encode specialist knowledge as if-then rules and sell widely.
  2. PromisesHuman-level intelligence is said to be close.
  3. Results lagThey cost a fortune to maintain and fail outside the rules.
  4. Money leavesCompanies stop buying. The market collapses by the early 1990s.

Reading a claim about next year? Ask which era’s evidence it rests on.

CIS137 · 1.2
Quick Check 1.2

It stunned the world. Was it learning?

Deep Blue beat the world chess champion in 1997. Explain why it still belongs to the rules era even though the result stunned the world.

Deep Blue searched about 200 million positions per second with scoring rules a team programmed. Its strength came from brute search and programmed judgment, not from learning.

CIS137 · 1.3
The one rule

If you would not post it on a public bulletin board, do not type it into an AI tool.

  • a password
  • a student ID
  • a Social Security number
  • your address, health, finances, phone number
  • anyone else’s personal information
  • a classmate’s work
  • an employer’s confidential data
CIS137 · 1.3
Quick Check 1.3

Your manager says: paste it in.

Your manager asks you to paste a customer’s email into a chat assistant to draft a reply. Using the rule, say what you would do instead, or what you would have to remove and confirm before you did it.

The email is another person’s information, so it fails the bulletin board test. Remove the customer’s name, contact details, and account facts first, and confirm your employer allows the tool. Or skip the paste and draft from a summary you write yourself.
