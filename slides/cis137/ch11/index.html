Quick Check 11.1

“The one my nephew uses.”
Which card fields first?

A plumbing contractor's office manager says the right tool is "the one my nephew uses." The job handles customers' home addresses. Using scenario alignment, name the two Tool Survey card fields you would check first, and justify each in one sentence.

Data policy: home addresses put the job on the sheet’s privacy and What never goes in lines, so check what the tool keeps and whether it trains on your input. Last tested: someone using it is not a test, so check whether it produced the required output on this task. Tier and cost or Access also works as the second field when the sentence ties it to a sheet line.

CIS137 · 11.3
A plumbing warranty claim

Three yes answers, no fix left: no AI here.

  1. The input testMust something from “What never goes in” go in for the job to work?YesThe claim rests on the photos of the customer's home and the payment record
  2. The decision testDoes the output commit someone to a decision that a tool cannot answer for?YesApproving the claim commits the company to a repair at its own cost
  3. The cost testDoes the no-AI option beat every candidate on time and risk?YesA person reads one claim against the warranty terms and the install record in about 15 minutes

A fix works when removing the input or letting a person decide still leaves a job. Here it does not.

No AI here Decline: this tool failed this job. No AI here: no AI tool should get it.

Quick Check 11.3

Pasted bank statements: a tool, a fix, or no AI here?

A small accounting firm wants a chat assistant to total each client's deductible expenses from pasted bank statements. Run the three tests, and decide whether the job gets a tool, a tool with a fix, or no AI here.

Input: yes. The totals rest on the statements, a client’s payment records. Decision: yes. Calling an expense deductible commits the firm. Cost: leans yes. Once a person makes every deductible call, the sum is a spreadsheet job. A fix would have to strip the payment records the totals rest on, or have a person decide every line, and either one removes the job: no AI here.

Quick Check 11.4

“Tool A is great.”
Rewrite it so a colleague can check it.

A Why line reads "Tool A is great and the team likes it." Critique the line, and rewrite it from the triage pilot's counts.

“Great” and “likes” are opinions with no count, so a colleague has nothing to check. A two-sentence rewrite: “Tool A matched the key in all 3 runs (8 of 8 categories, 8 of 8 urgency), while Tool B marked ticket 4 urgent in all 3. No review was timed, so time saved is still unknown.”
