Evaluation criteria

“Sounds professional” is a hope. “Under 120 words” is a criterion.

Checkable
Passes or fails on evidence you can point to.
Tied to the job
Comes from what the reader needs.
Few
Three to six. Ten turn every run into an audit.
Ranked
The must-pass criteria are marked.

Write them before the first run. A draft pulls your standards toward itself.

Seven steps. One change per lap.

  5. 5Change one thing
  6. 6Run again
  7. 7Stop when the criteria are met

Quick Check 6.2

One run, one word under the limit. Keep?

A new version scored one criterion higher than the last, in a single run, and its word count sits one word under the limit. Decide what you do before you mark it keep, and explain why.

Run the same version again in a fresh conversation first. Runs vary by chance, so one run is a weak test of a small gain, and a score sitting right at a limit can tip on the next run.

When to stop

Stop at good enough. Perfect has no criteria.

  1. The criteria are met in a fresh run and a re-run.
  2. Two versions in a row gained nothing.
  3. The remaining fix is faster by hand: “Thank you for being a valued client!”
  4. Prompting cannot fix the failure. Say so and stop.
