      The ideas behind the game

      Follow startup evidence before changing hardware. A dark preview is not proof of a stopped PC. Change one part at a time when isolating a fault. A degraded mirrored array and a separate backup provide different kinds of protection.

      This fictional station has independent live sources and a separate archive. Crew costs and six-block events are game rules, not repair-time estimates. RAID failure tolerance depends on its level. Here, a two-drive mirror has already lost one drive.

      WELCOME TO CHANNEL 04

      Make a little television.

      1. Choose one program. Live shows use crew. Recorded shows need working playback but leave both crew free.
      2. Assign spare crew. Read evidence, recover playback, protect recordings, or isolate the preview fault. You can leave crew unassigned.
      3. Air the block. The program runs first. Jobs finish second. Scheduled events happen last.
      4. Read what changed. Use the notebook and shift log. Try a new plan next block, or rewind any decision.

      Your aim: six filled blocks, a story, music, a film, and a verified separate archive copy. An imperfect night still reaches the closing credits.

      No countdown. No grades. Sound starts off. All controls work by keyboard or tap.
