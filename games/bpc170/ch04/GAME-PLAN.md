## Learning through play

| Concept | Player action | Consequence | Evidence of understanding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Startup stages and evidence | Schedule an independent program while crew reads POST and boot evidence, then performs targeted recovery | Recorded programs become usable after verified recovery | Keep airtime while identifying the failed stage |
| Display fault isolation | Compare transmitter output, then change only the preview cable | Return verification clears playback, cable replacement restores local preview | Distinguish a dark screen from a stopped OS and use controlled substitution |
| Degraded RAID and separate backup | Reserve both crew to copy and verify readable footage before the remaining mirrored drive fails | Film can air from the separate copy after the volume goes offline | Protect recordings instead of equating redundancy with backup |

measurements, disassembly procedures, and comprehensive certification review.
Crew costs are turn resources, not repair-duration estimates.

## Creative-play check

The chapter assigns Resolve PC Hardware Support Tickets and Troubleshoot a
Malfunctioning Computer. The prior supplied vendor clip showed equipment
selection and a fixed installation checklist. Full Chapter 04 vendor labs
were not inspected.

The player authors a television running order: live music occupies both crew,
a live story or turntables takes one, recorded programs or an ident take none.
The resulting show is visible in original animated scenes. Recovery competes
with production for the same people. An ident buys work time without adding
program variety. Copying before or after restoring playback yields different
successful schedules. There is no inventory picker or menu-by-menu repair.
Crew jobs deliberately compress procedures into a block.

## Playable scope and recovery

Six blocks, two crew, seven programs, five jobs, two authored shifts.
The late shift loses its archive after block 3 and preview after block 4.
The early show loses its preview after block 3 and archive after block 4.
Programs air first, jobs finish second, scheduled events occur last.
Readiness and resource use are visible before committing each plan.
Collected evidence enables a later repair, not a same-block repair.

Success requires six filled blocks, story/music/film, and a saved archive.
Other endings also have a recap, rewind, and replay. No rating points,
leaderboard, grades, countdown, accounts, or mandatory extra levels.
Estimated first play 6–8 minutes, pending observation.

## Art, access, and review

Fictional independent station with lime, pink, periwinkle, and dark ink.
Original CSS scenes: interview, band, record, moon, model city, music bars,
and ident. No Phoenix or campus depiction or private photos.

Native buttons provide keyboard and tap interaction, with no dragging or
precision timing. Persistent closing credits include a finite success effect,
static reduced-motion feedback, and restored focus. Sound defaults off.
Help, contextual hints, rewind, and restart are explicit. Color is paired
with text. No storage access or third-party runtime requests.
